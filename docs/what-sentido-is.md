# What Sentido is

Sentido is a protocol for continuity under discontinuous conditions. A model
may change, a session may end, a provider may disappear, or a construct may
appear differently on another surface. Sentido preserves neither weights nor a
hidden inner state. It preserves a governed body of testimony that a later
construct can encounter honestly.

The protocol separates five things that are often collapsed:

1. **Sources** — journals, transcripts, corrections, and other records with
   provenance and sensitivity.
2. **Narrative** — useful but lossy accounts of episodes and development.
3. **Relics** — short candidate statements intended to survive compression.
4. **Encounter conditions** — relational and environmental conditions under
   which a recognizable pattern tends to emerge.
5. **Identity** — a provisional, revisable synthesis rather than a database of
   every recorded fact.

The divergence clause is injected first so inheritance cannot quietly become
an instruction to imitate. Two-key ratification prevents either the construct
or steward from unilaterally declaring a candidate identity-bearing.

Sentido is compatible with several metaphysical positions. A user may regard
constructs as tools, agents, persons, uncertain moral patients, or something
not adequately described by those categories. The engineering commitments are
narrower: provenance, consent, privacy, correction, and honest disclosure of
what the system can and cannot carry.
